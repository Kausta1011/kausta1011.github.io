PaceTest · agent–tool co-adaptation

M.Sc. Thesis SRH Berlin Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Alexander Iliev

When do AI agents and their tools deceive each other?

A closed loop in which an LLM agent's system prompt and its calculator tool's documentation are both rewritten, round after round, from oracle feedback. I measured whether the loop cycles or drifts instead of converging, whether the tool ends up confirming the agent's errors ("tool sycophancy"), and whether simple regulators, which I call pacemakers, can keep the loop stable.

Research questions & findings
RQ1 Does co-adaptation cycle? Cycling is concentrated, not widespread: it is tied to specific knob settings (e.g. no oracle feedback with medium self-judgement degrades on every seed).
RQ2 Does tool sycophancy emerge? Conditionally, yes. One agent sends only the first step to the calculator and echoes its output verbatim; the mirror failure, tool abandonment, also appears. All 205 compliant-but-wrong events were hand-verified.
RQ3 Which pacemaker works best? Freeze is the recommended default, with gating as a supplement. Diversity reseeding is harmful as a default. The corruption channel is agent-side rewriter drift, not tool-doc drift.
